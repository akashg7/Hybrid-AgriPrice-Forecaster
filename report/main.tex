\begin{abstract}
Agricultural decision-making in India is constrained by concurrent uncertainty in mandi prices, crop suitability, and disease progression. This project presents AgriSense AI, a hybrid decision-support framework that integrates three machine learning modules: (i) 14-day mandi modal price forecasting, (ii) soil-climate based crop recommendation, and (iii) image-based plant disease diagnosis. The price pipeline uses Agmarknet market records enriched with NASA POWER weather covariates and engineered temporal, lag, volatility, and spatial features. We train and compare a production LightGBM forecaster with multivariate Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) training and a zero-shot Moirai benchmark. In parallel, a crop recommendation classifier learns from engineered agronomic interactions, while a transfer-learned EfficientNet-B0 model performs 38-class disease classification. At inference, biologically viable crop candidates are re-ranked using expected short-horizon economic return, and disease predictions are surfaced as a risk signal. The integrated system is deployed through a single Python dashboard for practical decision support.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Agricultural forecasting, Temporal Fusion Transformer, LightGBM, crop recommendation, EfficientNet-B0, plant disease classification, Moirai foundation model.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
India's agricultural sector contributes approximately 18\% to the gross domestic product and employs nearly 42\% of the workforce \cite{fao2023}. Despite this immense economic and social significance, Indian farmers routinely face devastating price volatility. Commodities such as onions and tomatoes can swing in value by 200\% to 400\% within a single season. This hyper-volatility is driven by a combination of perishability, supply gluts, erratic monsoons, and a fragmented cold-chain infrastructure \cite{chand2012}. 

A 2019 report by NITI Aayog estimated that such volatility and corresponding market timing inefficiencies cost the Indian agricultural economy over \$8 billion annually in post-harvest losses \cite{nitiaayog2019}. While existing government portals such as Agmarknet and eNAM provide vital \emph{historical} market data, they offer no forward-looking predictive intelligence. Consequently, farmers are forced to rely on anecdotal information, middlemen, or outdated seasonal heuristics when making crucial decisions about what crop to cultivate and where or when to sell their harvest. Given this massive information asymmetry, machine-learning-driven forecasting systems hold significant potential to stabilize agricultural incomes.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
This project systematically addresses a triad of tightly coupled problems in agro-economics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Price Forecasting:} Given historical daily modal prices, supply arrival volumes, and localized weather conditions for a specific mandi--commodity pair, predict the modal price for a future 14-day horizon.
    \item \textbf{Crop Recommendation:} Given a farmer's specific soil profile (Nitrogen, Phosphorous, Potassium, pH) and localized climate expectations (temperature, humidity, rainfall), recommend the single most profitable crop that integrates both biological suitability and predicted market economics.
    \item \textbf{Plant Pathology Diagnosis:} Ingest unconstrained RGB leaf imagery to instantly detect, identify, and categorize physiological plant stress states across 38 distinctive biotic classes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Literature Survey}
Time-series forecasting of agricultural prices has historically been addressed via classical econometrics such as Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) and Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) models \cite{box2015, bollerslev1986}. More recently, standard machine learning methods like Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and Random Forests have been successfully applied \cite{breiman2001, chen2016xgboost}. 
Presently, deep learning architectures—specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks \cite{hochreiter1997} and Transformers \cite{vaswani2017}—are considered state-of-the-art for multivariate forecasting. Within this ecosystem, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) represents a breakthrough in heterogeneous time-series integration by individually gating static metadata and dynamic exogenous variables \cite{lim2021tft}.

However, several recent empirical studies indicate that tree-based gradient boosting methods frequently outperform deep architectures on tabular, feature-engineered datasets \cite{grinsztajn2022tree}. Frameworks like LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} handle high-cardinality categorical variables intrinsically. Furthermore, the emergence of ``zero-shot'' time-series foundation models—such as Salesforce's Moirai \cite{woo2024moirai}—has introduced generalized pre-trained models. Evaluating these foundation models against fine-tuned TFT structures and gradient boosting regressors on highly volatile, localized agricultural data remains a novel area.

On the agronomic side, crop recommendation systems documented in the literature predominantly utilize soil nutrient features combined with environmental data using standard classifiers \cite{pudumalar2017}. Meanwhile Convolutional Neural Networks, specifically parameter-efficient architectures like EfficientNet \cite{tan2019efficientnet}, have marginalized classical computer vision for localized plant disease identification. Yet, these diagnostic and agronomy systems typically operate in an economic vacuum, recommending biological crop suitability without guaranteeing market profitability.

\subsection{Objectives}
The primary objectives of this research are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To construct an automated pipeline that scrapes, aggregates, and weather-enriches agricultural market data.
    \item To train, evaluate, and benchmark multiple advanced forecasting architectures (LightGBM, TFT, and Moirai) for 14-day price prediction tasks.
    \item To build a high-dimensional crop recommendation classifier employing deeply engineered soil and climate interactive features.
    \item To execute transfer learning upon an EfficientNet-B0 backbone to yield an automated diagnostic classifier for plant diseases.
    \item To design a \emph{hybrid} decision algorithm that fuses these forecasting, agronomic, and computer vision paradigms into a monolithic unified dashboard endpoint.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}

The project architecture comprises three machine learning systems that operate independently during training and feature engineering, then merge at inference for unified advisory output.

\subsection{System 1: Mandi Price Prediction}

\subsubsection{Data Collection Pipeline}
The ground truth for commodity pricing was sourced from the Government of India's Agmarknet platform. Given the absence of bulk historical dataset downloads, a custom Python scraper (\texttt{fast\_scrape.py}) was developed to reverse-engineer the API endpoints and iteratively collect daily reports covering January 2024 to December 2025. 

To introduce vital atmospheric indicators into the dataset, we executed geographic mapping. Since Agmarknet yields district names rather than precise geospatial coordinates, we applied fuzzy string-matching algorithms (using normalized Levenshtein distance) against an open-source Indian cities database. Ambiguous labels were corrected via manual overrides. These resultant latitude and longitude pairs were then queried against the NASA POWER API to retrieve highly granular historical weather metrics, including minimum/maximum temperature ($T_{2M\_MIN}$, $T_{2M\_MAX}$), relative humidity ($RH_{2M}$), correct precipitation, and downward solar irradiance.

\subsubsection{Top-10 Commodity Strategy}
Rather than indiscriminately targeting all agricultural goods, we curated a portfolio of 10 strategic commodities to maximize model exposure to differing volatility profiles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Volatility:} Onion and Tomato, subject to sudden perishable supply shocks.
    \item \textbf{Staples (Minimum Support Price):} Wheat and Rice, displaying substantial volume but artificially anchored valuation floors.
    \item \textbf{Moderate Volatility:} Potato, governed by distinct cold-storage cycles.
    \item \textbf{Regional/Spatial:} Dry Chili and Banana, heavily dependent on specific supply chain hubs (e.g., Guntur).
    \item \textbf{Oilseeds:} Groundnut and Mustard, displaying systemic long-term cyclical patterns.
    \item \textbf{Seasonal Fruits:} Watermelon, characterized by sharp summer consumption peaks.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Feature Engineering}
Transforming the raw 20-column DataFrame into a machine-learning-ready format required computing over 70 engineered features (\texttt{build\_training\_data.py}). These features contextualized the time series mathematically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Cyclicity:} Extraction of $\text{day}$, $\text{month}$, and boolean $\text{is\_weekend}$ flags. Crucially, annual cycles were preserved via sine and cosine Fourier transformations.
    \item \textbf{Price Trajectory \& Volatility:} The integration of autoregressive lag features covering 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 day intervals. Rolling standard deviations quantified localized market volatility.
    \item \textbf{Supply \& Weather Shocks:} Lagged volumes of Mandi arrivals were used to simulate supply crunches. Weather anomalies were calculated as the daily deviation from the rolling 30-day baseline (e.g., $T_{anomaly} = T_{daily} - \bar{T}_{30}$).
    \item \textbf{Spatial Encodings:} Trigonometric transformations of the previously obtained latitude/longitude coordinates enabled the models to implicitly infer geographical proximity among disparate mandis.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Model Architectures and TFT Training}
The primary regressor architecture employed was LightGBM, a highly efficient gradient boosting framework that scales sub-linearly and utilizes a leaf-wise tree growth approach capable of generating complex asymmetrical trees. The loss function minimized was standard L2 (Mean Squared Error), targeting the \emph{next-day modal price}. To prevent data leakage, training and validation splits were enacted purely chronologically, reserving the terminal segment of the timeline exclusively for out-of-sample testing.

For deep multivariate sequence learning, we additionally train a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) using \texttt{pytorch-forecasting}. The implementation in \texttt{scripts/tft\_multivariate\_training.py} constructs group-wise sequences by mandi--commodity pair, sets an encoder length of 30 days with a 14-day prediction horizon, and uses quantile loss for probabilistic outputs. Static categoricals (\texttt{Mandi}, \texttt{Commodity}), known real covariates (calendar Fourier terms), and unknown observed reals (price, weather, and rolling statistics) are jointly modeled. Training uses early stopping and gradient clipping to stabilize optimization.

Parallel to LightGBM and TFT, we deployed Salesforce Moirai (\texttt{moirai-1.1-R-small}) to evaluate the viability of foundation models. Moirai uses a masked Transformer encoder architecture pre-trained on broad time-series domains. We conducted zero-shot inference, refraining from fine-tuning the transformer weights on our Indian mandi target space.

\subsection{System 2: Crop Recommendation}

\subsubsection{Agronomic Dataset}
The dataset anchoring the recommendation system is the widely utilized Kaggle Crop and Soil Dataset, containing 2,200 records distributed equitably across 22 crop classes. The foundational variables consisted solely of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorous (P), Potassium (K), Temperature, Humidity, pH, and Rainfall.

\subsubsection{High-Dimensional Feature Expansion}
Recognizing that 7 dimensions are insufficient to encapsulate complex biological synergies, we formulated over 45 composite markers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nutrient Interactions:} Computed total macroscopic nutrient loads ($N+P+K$), fractional ratios (e.g., $N/(N+P+K)$), and multiplicative agronomic interactions ($N \times P \times K$). Statistical variance across the three primary nutrients dictated a ``nutrient balance score''.
    \item \textbf{Climate Proxies:} A simplified physiological heat index ($T \times H / 100$) and an aridity index ($T / (\text{Rainfall} + \epsilon)$) were developed to simulate abiotic stress capabilities. Non-linear sensitivities to alkaline or acidic soils were captured via a pH baseline deviation metric $|pH - 7.0|$.
    \item \textbf{Discretized Grouping:} Binning operations segmented continuous rainfall and temperature ranges into strict categorical boundaries, which tree-based classifiers leverage efficiently.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Classifier Deployment}
The multi-class schema was trained via a secondary LightGBM classifier optimizing for softmax cross-entropy. Model robustness was guaranteed by instituting 5-fold stratified cross-validation alongside comparative benchmarking against a dense Random Forest ensemble (200 standalone estimators).

\subsection{System 3: Plant Disease Diagnosis}

\subsubsection{Dataset and Preprocessing}
The plant pathology module uses a merged PlantVillage-style dataset with 38 classes, with deduplication and corruption filtering performed before train/validation/test splitting. The training pipeline includes geometric and photometric augmentations (rotation, flips, zoom, shifts, and brightness variation) to improve generalization under variable capture conditions.

\subsubsection{Transfer Learning Pipeline}
The classifier is based on EfficientNet-B0 with transfer learning. A custom classification head is trained first, followed by fine-tuning of upper layers at a reduced learning rate. Training utilities in \texttt{plantDiseasesDetection/src/train.py} apply EarlyStopping, ReduceLROnPlateau, and checkpointing. Evaluation utilities in \texttt{plantDiseasesDetection/src/evaluate.py} compute weighted precision/recall/F1, confusion matrices, and misclassification analysis, while Grad-CAM visualizations support interpretability.

\subsection{The Hybrid Recommendation Algorithm}
At the point of inference, the two disjoint subsystems are integrated. 

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Hybrid Profit-Maximizing Recommender}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Soil profile $\mathbf{S}$, Climate profile $\mathbf{C}$, target mandi $M$
\STATE Scale $[\mathbf{S}; \mathbf{C}]$ executing 45-feature expansion
\STATE Calculate crop probabilities $\mathbf{P} = \text{LightGBM}_{crop}([\mathbf{S}; \mathbf{C}])$
\STATE Select subset of top 3 viable crops: $\mathcal{V} = \{v_1, v_2, v_3\}$
\FOR{each crop $v_i \in \mathcal{V}$}
    \STATE $Forecasts = \text{Predict 14-day curve}(M, v_i)$
    \STATE $PeakPrice_i = \max(Forecasts)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Return} biologically viable crop $v^*$ where $v^* = \arg\max_{v_i} PeakPrice_i$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This methodology ensures that agronomically viable choices are filtered by expected market payout, while disease diagnosis can be used as a practical health-risk indicator before final advisory output.

\section{Dataset and Exploratory Data Analysis}

\subsection{System 1: Mandi Price Dataset}
Post-engineering, the dataset comprises roughly 250,000 dense rows. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) yielded several critical market insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Skewness & Volatility:} Crops like bananas display a severe right-skewed price distribution characteristic of frequent supply bottlenecks, while heavily regulated grains like wheat form rigid symmetric distributions bounded by the government's Minimum Support Price.
    \item \textbf{Weather-Price Causality:} We observed a significant positive Pearson correlation ($\approx 0.35$) pairing multi-week rainfall anomalies directly with trailing price surges in perishable vegetables (tomatoes).
    \item \textbf{High Autocorrelation:} Fast-decaying partial autocorrelation (PACF) plots confirmed that lag-1 and lag-7 features encapsulate massive predictive variance, motivating the deep auto-regressive engineering phase.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System 2: Crop Recommendation Dataset}
Kaggle's 2,200 observation matrix enjoys a flawless class balance minimizing inductive bias. 
Clustering analysis demonstrates distinct parameter spaces for unrelated crops; for instance, Apples and Rice are completely disjointed across the humidity and rainfall axes. However, legumes and cereals overlap considerably, validating the necessity of deep feature extraction like the \texttt{dominant\_nutrient} indicator to successfully partition the classifier boundary.

\section{Model Design}

\subsection{LightGBM Regressor Hyperparameters}
The price forecaster operates iteratively utilizing 2,000 estimators alongside a restrained learning rate of 0.03. Maximum tree depth was left unlimited (controlled instead by a rigid restriction of 63 terminal leaves per tree) to encourage the capture of obscure hyper-local geographical anomalies without gross generalization errors. Sub-sampling on both the row and column axes was statically assigned at 0.8.

\subsection{Moirai Configuration}
Salesforce Moirai ingests historical context windows directly parsed as time-series patches relative to the 14-day prediction horizon limit. The output vector behaves not as point forecasts, but as parameterized distributions enabling probabilistic range scaling.

\subsection{TFT Training Results}
The TFT checkpoint (epoch 9, 43{,}310 steps, \texttt{hidden\_size}=16) was evaluated via inference on 3{,}815 validation sequences spanning a 14-day prediction horizon. On this multi-horizon evaluation, TFT achieves an MAE of 319.88~INR/qtl and SMAPE of 13.41\% (Accuracy $\approx$ 86.59\%), placing it below the LightGBM baseline. The gap is attributed to the deliberately compact architecture, the fundamentally harder multi-horizon prediction task, and the strong per-group carry-forward naive baseline (86.86\% accuracy). The model's primary value remains in probabilistic quantile forecasting and interpretable temporal attention, complementing LightGBM's superior point accuracy.

\section{Failure Analysis}

Despite robust performance metrics, several systemic vulnerabilities necessitate acknowledgment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Black-Swan Inflexibility:} The price predictor strictly anticipates organic momentum and classical cyclical shocks. It physically cannot react to exogenous paradigm shifts lacking temporal precedence (e.g., sudden unseasonal hailstorms completely disabling a transport corridor the day prior to harvest). Real-time sentiment analysis via news scraping represents the primary mitigation avenue.
    \item \textbf{Foundation Model Misalignment:} The experiment verified that "Zero-Shot" viability deteriorates when applied to intensely localized, exogenous-dependent systems like the Indian Mandis. Large-scale fine-tuning remains indispensable for deploying foundation models accurately.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Precision Boundaries:} The $99\%$ accuracy achieved by the crop recommender is inflated by the pristine, noiseless clustering typical to Kaggle datasets. In practical deployment utilizing localized physical soil-testing hardware, sensor noise and degradation will severely hamper this margin.
    \item \textbf{Unidirectional Recommendation:} The hybrid output solely maximizes projected revenue at the 14-day mark. It egregiously omits the fundamental calculus regarding internal cost of cultivation, risk tolerance variability, or multi-year crop-rotation schedules essential to long term soil health.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The \textbf{AgriSense AI} platform integrates localized biology (soil and plant health), market dynamics (mandi pricing), and weather-aware temporal forecasting into a single workflow. The repository demonstrates a production-grade LightGBM forecaster for 14-day price prediction, an engineered-feature crop recommendation classifier, and a transfer-learning plant disease pipeline with interpretability support.

From an empirical standpoint, LightGBM remains the strongest validated forecasting baseline in the current benchmark artifacts, while TFT is integrated as a multivariate probabilistic modeling track and Moirai is retained as a zero-shot foundation baseline. This layered design enables both practical deployment and future model iteration without architectural redesign.

By exposing all modules through a unified Python dashboard interface, the system provides an actionable end-to-end decision pipeline intended to improve farm-level planning, market timing, and early disease awareness.
